FIRESTARTERS combat is built on a single rule: keep moving or die. Studio Kerosene's arcade movement shooter throws you into STEEL, a broadcast gladiator bloodsport, with rocket skates instead of cover and android corpses instead of health packs. The systems are already live in Steam Early Access as of October 28, 2026, and this breakdown covers what is actually confirmed about how fighting works right now — and what remains subject to change.

## Movement Is the Core Combat System

In most arena shooters, movement is how you get to the fight. In FIRESTARTERS, movement *is* the fight. Kera's kit pairs a performance-enhancing STEEL Battlesuit with a custom-built pair of rocket skates, and the official description frames the arenas as a skatepark rather than a series of rooms.

The verified movement verbs are:

- **Skate** — your baseline traversal across open arena floor
- **Slide** — low, fast repositioning
- **Grind** — traversal along the rails that run through each space
- **Ground pound** — a downward attack option

The Steam listing describes the design goal as "best-in-class character movement" that rewards players willing to master the controls. A hands-on preview from Coloured Static Press noted that the arenas — bright training spaces and red-lit industrial interiors alike — are built to be traversed, not used as cover.

That distinction matters for combat decisions. If an arena has no safe corner, then disengaging means finding a rail line or a ramp, not backing into a wall. Momentum is your defensive stat.

## Six Weapons, Zero Pickups

FIRESTARTERS arms Kera with what the store page calls an "expert-curated suite of Arena FPS weaponry" — six weapons total. The number is confirmed; the individual weapon roster, damage values, and ammo capacities are not detailed in the available material, so treat any specific tier list you see elsewhere as unverified.

The bigger mechanical twist is the resource economy. There are **no health, armor, or ammunition pickups** anywhere in a STEEL Arena. Instead, resources come from defeated androids. The official framing is blunt: if the world doesn't give, you take.

Lead developer Alex Harte summarized the intent in an October 2026 announcement covered by MonsterVine: "There are no safety nets in the arenas of Steel. Hunting is the path to survival."

Practically, that means aggression is not just encouraged — it is the only way to refill. A player who plays passively runs out of ammunition and health simultaneously. Contestants who play with skill and strategy, per the developers, end up with more resources than they strictly need.

## Enemies, Arenas, and Readability

Your opposition is an android horde, and the arenas themselves are hostile. The Steam page specifically calls out "savage mechanized claws" as a hazard to avoid while skating through each space. Combined with rails, ramps, and sharp turns, the environment demands constant spatial awareness.

One caveat worth flagging from hands-on coverage: Coloured Static Press reported that the dense HUD, stark visual effects, and multiple simultaneous warnings can compete for your attention when the action accelerates. The reviewer praised the clarity of the score feedback but suggested that arena readability will be equally important going forward. That is one outlet's short-session impression of the Early Access build, not a verdict on the finished game.

## Scoring, Storms, and Arcade Mode

Clearing an encounter is the floor, not the ceiling. FIRESTARTERS tracks remaining enemies, score, and multipliers during a run, and the game is explicit that spectators in STEEL won't tolerate stagnation — you are expected to kill faster and score higher, not merely survive.

The loop works like this:

1. Complete a Storm (a combat encounter) for the first time in Story Mode.
2. That Storm becomes replayable in Arcade Mode.
3. Arcade runs post an official high score, letting you chase personal bests, then Steam friends, then the global leaderboard.

Arcade Mode is unlockable and included in Episode One. Because scoring rewards sustained pace rather than cautious clears, the scoring layer feeds directly back into the movement design — a clean run is one where you never stop lining up the next threat.

## What Early Access Actually Contains

Studio Kerosene describes the base gameplay, story, and combat systems as "already complete and functional," while noting they remain subject to change as new content ships. Episode One is the current build, and four episodes are planned before version 1.0, with an estimated 18 to 24 months in Early Access.

Two practical notes for anyone weighing a purchase:

- Early Access buyers receive all subsequent content updates before full release at no extra cost, though the price is planned to rise as content ships.
- The game runs on a custom in-house engine, which the developer says means no AI upscaling and no shader compilation stutter. Those are Studio Kerosene's claims, not independently verified findings.

You can check the current build status and system requirements on the [official FIRESTARTERS Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3733710/FIRESTARTERS/).

## FAQ

**Is FIRESTARTERS combat more like a shooter or a movement game?**

Both, deliberately. It is described as an arcade movement shooter, and the two halves are inseparable because there are no pickups — you have to physically reach downed androids to restock, which keeps shooting and skating tied together.

**How many weapons are in FIRESTARTERS combat?**

Six. The Steam listing confirms a six-weapon arsenal, though the individual weapons and their stats have not been detailed in the available official material.

**Do you really get no health or ammo pickups?**

Correct. STEEL Arenas contain no health, armor, or ammunition pickups. Resources come from defeated android enemies instead, which the developers say rewards skilled, aggressive play.

**Can I practice FIRESTARTERS combat without the story?**

Yes, once you unlock it. After clearing a Storm for the first time in Story Mode, you can replay it in Arcade Mode and compete for high scores on global leaderboards.
