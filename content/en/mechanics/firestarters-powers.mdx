If you searched for FIRESTARTERS powers expecting a menu full of unlockable super moves, the Early Access build has a different answer: Studio Kerosene's arcade shooter does not document a powers system, an ability tree, or cooldown-based specials. What Kera actually gets is a fixed kit — a performance-enhancing STEEL Battlesuit, a custom pair of rocket skates, and six weapons — backed by a resource loop that turns defeated androids into the health, armor, and ammo you would normally pick up off the floor. Below is what is confirmed for Episode One, what a hands-on preview actually observed on screen, and what remains pure speculation for later episodes.

## The Short Answer: Does FIRESTARTERS Have a Powers System?

Not in the way the term usually implies. The [official FIRESTARTERS Steam listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3733710/FIRESTARTERS/) describes Kera's loadout in equipment terms — a battlesuit, rocket skates, and "an expert-curated suite of Arena FPS weaponry" — and never mentions abilities, skill points, upgrades, or unlockable moves. Launch coverage from [MonsterVine](https://monstervine.com/2026/10/firestarters-steam-early-access-october-28/) and [Noisy Pixel](https://noisypixel.net/firestarters-steam-early-access-october-28/) repeats the same framing: movement tools plus guns, not a power fantasy with tiers.

That matters because it changes how you should approach the game. There is no build to plan and no ability to unlock before a run. Your "power" is entirely mechanical skill with the movement system and resource economy the developers built into Episode One.

One caveat: the game is in Early Access, and the developers explicitly say the base gameplay, story, and combat systems are "subject to change as new content is developed." Nothing here is a permanent guarantee.

## Rocket Skates and the Battlesuit Are the Actual Powers

If any single element deserves the label, it's mobility. The Steam page lists four named movement actions — skate, slide, grind, and ground pound — and describes STEEL arenas as being repurposed into skateparks full of rails, ramps, and sharp turns rather than cover-shooting corridors.

In practice, that means:

- **Skating and sliding** carry your momentum across open arena floors.
- **Grinding** turns rails into traversal lines rather than obstacles.
- **Ground pound** gives you a vertical finisher to slam back into the fight.
- **The battlesuit** is the in-fiction justification for all of it, described as performance-enhancing gear rather than an upgradable stat block.

A [Coloured Static Press hands-on preview](https://colouredstaticpress.com/firestarters-hands-on-preview-steam-early-access-studio-kerosene/) published October 30, 2026, described the game as wanting momentum above all else, with arenas designed to be traversed instead of used as cover. That is the closest thing to a "power" the game currently offers — and it is available from the first minute.

## Six Weapons, No Pickups, and a Self-Sufficient Gunplay Loop

The second half of Kera's kit is the arsenal. Steam lists six weapons used against an android horde, and the store description stresses "fully self-sufficient gunplay" — meaning the guns are expected to carry you without scavenging.

The twist is how resources work. FIRESTARTERS places no health, armor, or ammunition pickups in a STEEL arena. Instead, you take what you need from enemies you kill. Lead developer Alex Harte framed it bluntly in launch coverage: there are no safety nets in the arenas, and hunting is the path to survival.

That design choice is effectively a passive power. Aggression is rewarded, retreating is punished, and every kill is a resupply. Players who play with skill and strategy are told they will end up with more resources than they strictly need.

## Score, Multipliers, and the HUD Element Previews Noticed

Episode One ships with two modes: the first part of Story Mode, and an unlockable Arcade Mode where completed Storms can be replayed for an official high score on global leaderboards. Beating your personal best, then your Steam friends, then the world is the stated progression loop.

During the Coloured Static Press session, the writer observed the HUD tracking remaining enemies, score, and multipliers, and referenced a "synchronisation meter" visible in their captured footage. Treat that meter cautiously — no official material explains what it measures or whether it modifies gameplay. The same preview also flagged that a dense HUD, stark visual effects, and simultaneous warnings can compete for your attention when the action accelerates, which is worth knowing before you chase high scores.

## FAQ

**Do FIRESTARTERS powers include super moves or an ability tree?**
No. Official materials describe equipment and movement only — battlesuit, rocket skates, and six weapons. No ability tree, skill points, or unlockable specials appear in any verified source for Episode One.

**How do you heal or restock ammo in FIRESTARTERS if there are no pickups?**
You take resources from enemies you defeat. STEEL arenas contain no health, armor, or ammunition pickups, so killing androids is the only resupply route — which pushes you to keep attacking rather than retreating.

**Are FIRESTARTERS powers tied to movement or to shooting?**
Both, but movement comes first. Skating, sliding, grinding, and ground pounding define how you survive the arenas, while the six-weapon arsenal handles the damage. The two systems are deliberately linked through the kill-to-resupply loop.

**Will more powers be added during Early Access?**
Nothing has been announced. Studio Kerosene plans four episodes over roughly 18 to 24 months and says combat systems may change as new content is developed, but no additional abilities have been confirmed.
