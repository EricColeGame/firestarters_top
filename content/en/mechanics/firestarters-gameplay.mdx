## What FIRESTARTERS Gameplay Actually Feels Like

FIRESTARTERS gameplay is built around one idea: never stop moving. Studio Kerosene's arcade movement shooter puts you on a pair of rocket skates inside a televised gladiator bloodsport, then strips away the safety nets that most shooters rely on. You play as Kera, a prisoner fighting for the Firestarters — the sport's newest team of inmates — and the only way to survive a match is to keep skating, keep shooting, and keep scoring.

The game launched into Steam Early Access on October 28, 2026, with Episode One. That means the base movement, story, and combat systems are already in place, but the studio plans to keep building for roughly 18 to 24 months. If you want to know what you're actually doing moment to moment before you buy, this is the loop.

## Skate, Slide, Grind, and Ground Pound

The arenas in STEEL are described by the developer as skateparks first and battlegrounds second. Every space is meant to be traversed rather than used as cover, and the movement vocabulary reflects that: you skate, slide, grind along rails, and ground pound your way through the environment while dodging mechanized claws.

That design changes how you read a room. In a hands-on preview published by Coloured Static Press on October 30, 2026, the writer noted that runs move between bright, sun-bleached training spaces and red-lit industrial interiors, both built from rails, ramps, and sharp turns. Their takeaway was that momentum matters more than caution — a clean run is about lining up the next target while maintaining speed, not hunkering down and clearing enemies one at a time.

If you're coming from a traditional arena shooter, expect an adjustment period. Standing still is the mistake the game punishes hardest.

## Combat: Six Weapons and No Pickups

Kera's loadout is a performance-enhancing battlesuit, custom rocket skates, and a suite of six weapons aimed at the android horde. The important mechanical twist is what you won't find lying around:

- **No health pickups.** There are no medkits in a STEEL arena.
- **No armor pickups.** Nothing to stack before a fight.
- **No ammunition drops.** You don't scavenge boxes between waves.

Instead, resources come from combat itself. The developer's framing is that contestants who play with skill and strategy end up with everything they need — you take what you need from defeated androids rather than waiting for the arena to hand it to you. Lead developer Alex Harte put it bluntly in an October 2026 announcement covered by MonsterVine: there are no safety nets in the arenas of Steel, and hunting is the path to survival.

That single design choice reshapes the whole fight. Aggression isn't a style preference; it's the resource economy. Backing off to recover doesn't work the way it does elsewhere, because recovery is something you earn by pushing forward.

## Storms, Arcade Mode, and the Score Chase

Clearing a Storm — the game's term for a combat encounter — is only half the job. STEEL is a livestreamed bloodsport run by a crime syndicate, and the audience demands entertainment. The game tracks remaining enemies, your score, and multipliers as you go, nudging you toward faster kills and cleaner routes rather than simple survival.

The structure works in two layers:

1. **Story Mode.** You play through the narrative with visual-novel-style cutscenes between fights, following Kera's attempt to earn freedom for her friends and atone for her past.
2. **Arcade Mode.** After you clear a Storm for the first time in the story, you can return to it in Arcade Mode and set an official high score — first against your own best, then against Steam friends, then potentially against the global leaderboard.

That replay loop is where the movement system pays off. A route that gets you through alive is very different from a route that gets you through fast, and the scoring system is designed to make you care about the difference.

One caveat worth flagging from the hands-on preview: the red industrial arenas have a strong visual identity, but the previewer found that a dense HUD, stark effects, and overlapping warnings can compete for your attention when the action speeds up. Readability of the arena itself is something to watch as episodes ship.

## FAQ

**Is FIRESTARTERS gameplay more shooter or more movement game?**

Both, but movement comes first. The arenas are designed as skateparks with rails, ramps, and grindable surfaces, and your survival depends on sustained momentum. Shooting is how you convert that momentum into resources and score.

**Can you heal in FIRESTARTERS?**

Not with pickups. There are no health, armor, or ammunition items placed in STEEL arenas. The developer's design intent is that you earn what you need by defeating androids and playing aggressively rather than retreating to recover.

**Do you need to finish the story before playing Arcade Mode?**

Arcade Mode unlocks per Storm. Once you clear a specific Storm for the first time in Story Mode, that Storm becomes available in Arcade Mode, where you can chase a personal best, compare against Steam friends, and push for the global leaderboard.

**Is FIRESTARTERS gameplay complete in Early Access?**

The base gameplay, story, and combat systems are described by the developer as complete and functional in Episode One, but they remain subject to change as new content is developed. Three more episodes are planned before version 1.0, arriving as free updates for Early Access owners.
