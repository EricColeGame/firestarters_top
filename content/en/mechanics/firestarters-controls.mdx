## What the Evidence Actually Confirms About FIRESTARTERS Controls

If you searched for FIRESTARTERS controls hoping for a clean key map, here is the honest answer: neither the official Steam listing nor any of the launch coverage published one. What the sources *do* confirm is the control vocabulary you will be learning — a movement set built around rocket skates, and a gunplay loop that runs on six weapons. The Steam page describes the game as granting "best-in-class character movement and fully self-sufficient gunplay to all players brave enough to master the controls."

That phrase matters. FIRESTARTERS, from Studio Kerosene, is an arcade movement shooter where the arenas are explicitly described as skateparks rather than cover-shooting galleries. Controls are the game, not a layer on top of it.

## The Movement Vocabulary: Skate, Slide, Grind, and Ground Pound

The official Steam description names four movement verbs directly: **skate, slide, grind, and ground pound**. These are the actions Kera's custom-built rocket skates are designed for, and the arenas are built from rails, ramps, and sharp turns to support them.

| Action | What's confirmed | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Skate | Rocket skates provide high-speed traversal | Base layer for everything else |
| Slide | Listed as a core traversal verb | Likely your low-profile option in tight spaces |
| Grind | Rails are a designed arena feature | Keeps momentum across gaps and elevations |
| Ground pound | Listed as a core traversal verb | A vertical commitment move with combat implications |

The Coloured Static Press hands-on preview supports the design intent: their runs moved through "vast arenas of rails, ramps and sharp turns," and they described spaces as "designed to be traversed rather than treated as rooms in which to take cover." Their takeaway was that the game "wants momentum."

Specific input bindings — which button grinds, which button pounds — are not documented in any source here. The authoritative place to check is the game's own settings screen, since Early Access builds can and do remap.

## Gunplay and the Six-Weapon Arsenal

Kera carries what the Steam page calls an "expert-curated suite of Arena FPS weaponry" — six weapons total, described as "devastating," aimed at an android horde. The critical control-adjacent detail is what you *don't* have:

- No health pickups in a STEEL Arena
- No armor pickups
- No ammunition drops

Instead, resources come from defeated enemies. Lead developer Alex Harte put it bluntly in MonsterVine's coverage: "There are no safety nets in the arenas of Steel. Hunting is the path to survival." In practice, that means your trigger discipline and target-priority decisions are part of the control scheme in a way they wouldn't be in a conventional shooter. You cannot retreat to a health pack; you have to push forward to resupply.

## How to Learn the FIRESTARTERS Controls Without Bad Habits

There's no official tutorial breakdown in the available material, so treat these as grounded starting points rather than a proven curriculum:

1. **Start in the training spaces.** The Coloured Static Press session moved between "bright, almost sun-bleached training spaces" and red-lit industrial arenas. The brighter spaces read as the lower-pressure environment for dialing in skate and grind timing.
2. **Learn one traversal verb at a time.** Skate is the foundation; grind and slide are modifiers on top of it. Trying to chain all four before any of them feels natural is how momentum games get frustrating.
3. **Practice resupply runs.** Since ammo and health come from kills, drill the habit of converting a defeated android into your next engagement rather than backing off.
4. **Don't chase score on your first clear.** The Steam page frames Arcade Mode as a return trip: finish a Storm in Story Mode first, then come back to set an official high score. Separating "learn the controls" from "chase the leaderboard" keeps both goals cleaner.

## Reading the Arena While You Move

The most useful control skill in FIRESTARTERS may be spatial awareness rather than any single input. The Coloured Static Press preview flagged a real tension here: the red industrial spaces have a strong identity, but "a dense HUD, stark effects and multiple simultaneous warnings can compete for the eye when the action accelerates."

Their gameplay capture also noted a synchronization meter on screen alongside tracked remaining enemies, score, and multipliers. If your eyes are locked on the score readout, you are not reading the rails. Their suggestion — that ensuring players can always read the arena will be as important as clear score feedback — is worth internalizing as a player too.

One practical note: the Steam page carries a photosensitivity warning for flashing lights and stroboscopic effects. If you are sensitive to that, factor it in before a long session.

## FAQ

**Are the default FIRESTARTERS controls published anywhere official?**
Not in the material reviewed. The Steam listing describes the movement set — skate, slide, grind, ground pound — and the six-weapon arsenal, but does not include a key map. Check the in-game settings menu for the current bindings, since Early Access builds can change.

**Can I rebind the FIRESTARTERS controls?**
That isn't confirmed by any source here. Remapping is common in PC shooters, but treat it as unverified until you see it in the options menu yourself.

**Do I need to learn all four movement abilities immediately?**
You don't have to. The game's own framing pushes players to master the controls over time, and the arenas reward momentum. Building up from basic skating to grinds and slides is a more sustainable path than trying to chain everything on your first Storm.

**Why do the FIRESTARTERS controls feel so different from other shooters?**
Because there are no health, armor, or ammo pickups in a STEEL Arena — you resupply by defeating androids. Your movement and your shooting are tied to the same aggressive loop, which is why mastering the controls is treated as a skill ceiling rather than a formality.
