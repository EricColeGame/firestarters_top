FIRESTARTERS doesn't hand you a skill tree — it hands you a pair of rocket skates and expects you to figure out the rest. Studio Kerosene's arcade movement shooter, in Steam Early Access since October 28, 2026, builds its entire toolkit out of traversal and gunplay rather than unlockable powers. So the "abilities" you'll actually use are skate, slide, grind, and ground pound, backed by six weapons and a resource system that only pays out when you kill something.

That distinction matters if you're coming in expecting a progression screen. Nothing in the launch material describes purchasable perks, cooldown powers, or character levels. What exists is a fixed, skill-based move set — and the game is balanced around how well you chain it.

## What "FIRESTARTERS Abilities" Actually Means

On the [official FIRESTARTERS Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3733710/FIRESTARTERS/), the developer describes the game as a "fast-paced arcade movement shooter" where Kera's equipment grants "best-in-class character movement and fully self-sufficient gunplay." That phrasing is the closest thing to an ability list the game offers at launch.

Studio Kerosene states that the base gameplay, story, and combat systems in Episode One are "already complete and functional, though they are subject to change as new content is developed." In practice, that means the move set below is what you're working with now — and it may expand across the planned four episodes.

Worth noting: FIRESTARTERS is a game about *Kera*, a prisoner fighting for the Firestarters in the bloodsport STEEL. Her abilities are diegetic — they come from her battlesuit and skates, not from a menu.

## Rocket-Skate Movement: Skate, Slide, Grind, and Ground Pound

The Steam listing names four verbs directly: **skate, slide, grind, and ground pound**. These are your primary abilities in any practical sense, and they're the reason arenas are built the way they are.

- **Skate** — your baseline. Rocket skates carry you across open floor at speed, which is how you close distance and dodge incoming attacks.
- **Slide** — a low, fast option for slipping under or past hazards and keeping momentum through tight geometry.
- **Grind** — rails are placed throughout arenas as traversal lines, not decoration. Grinding lets you cross a room without touching the floor.
- **Ground pound** — the one explicitly offensive movement tool named so far. It's a downward strike that turns verticality into damage.

A hands-on preview from [Coloured Static Press](https://colouredstaticpress.com/firestarters-hands-on-preview-steam-early-access-studio-kerosene/) described arenas of "rails, ramps and sharp turns" that are "designed to be traversed rather than treated as rooms in which to take cover." That's the key mental model: FIRESTARTERS has no cover shooter DNA. Standing still is how you die.

The same preview flagged one practical caveat — a dense HUD and multiple simultaneous on-screen warnings can compete for your attention when the pace spikes. Learning the arenas well enough to read them at speed is part of the skill curve.

## The STEEL Battlesuit, Rocket Skates, and Six Weapons

Kera's loadout is fixed and story-given. The Steam page describes three components: a performance-enhancing **STEEL Battlesuit**, a custom-built **pair of rocket skates**, and an "expert-curated suite of Arena FPS weaponry."

The weapon count is confirmed as **six**. Beyond that number, no official material names individual weapons, damage values, or firing modes — so treat any specific weapon list you see elsewhere with suspicion until Studio Kerosene publishes one.

What is confirmed is how the guns behave in relation to the world: they're described as "fully self-sufficient gunplay." You are not meant to run dry and go hunting for a crate. The arsenal is designed to keep functioning as long as you keep killing.

## Resource Harvesting Replaces Health and Ammo Pickups

This is the mechanic that reshapes how every other ability gets used. The Steam page states plainly: "You will find no health, armor, or ammunition pickups in a STEEL Arena."

Instead, resources come from defeated androids. Lead developer Alex Harte put it this way to [MonsterVine](https://monstervine.com/2026/10/firestarters-steam-early-access-october-28/): there are no safety nets in STEEL's arenas, and hunting is the path to survival.

The practical consequence is that aggression *is* your sustain ability. Retreating to recover isn't an option, because there's nothing to retreat to. Your rocket skates stop being pure mobility and start being a resource-acquisition tool — you skate toward the next android because that's where your next heal and your next bullets live.

## Momentum, Multipliers, and Arcade Mode Scoring

FIRESTARTERS tracks more than survival. Per hands-on impressions, the HUD surfaces remaining enemies, current score, and multipliers while you move through each arena. Combat encounters are called **Storms**.

Clearing a Storm is only the entry fee. The Steam page frames it as a performance sport: spectators "won't stand for stagnation," and competitors are pushed to play better, slay faster, and score higher. Once you finish a Storm for the first time in Story Mode, it unlocks in **Arcade Mode**, where you chase an official high score against Steam friends and a global leaderboard.

That scoring layer is what gives the movement abilities depth. A clean run isn't the run where you survive — it's the run where you keep the multiplier alive by never slowing down.

## FAQ

**Does FIRESTARTERS have a skill tree or unlockable abilities?**
No skill tree, perk system, or unlockable power set is described in any official launch material. FIRESTARTERS abilities are a fixed kit — skate, slide, grind, ground pound, and six weapons — that you improve at through practice rather than through progression. Studio Kerosene says combat systems may change as new episodes ship.

**Can you heal in FIRESTARTERS?**
Yes, but not from pickups. There are no health, armor, or ammunition pickups placed in STEEL arenas. You recover what you need from defeated androids, which pushes you to keep attacking instead of backing off.

**How many weapons are in FIRESTARTERS?**
Six. The Steam listing describes "an arsenal of six devastating weapons," though it doesn't name them individually or list their stats.

**Will FIRESTARTERS abilities change during Early Access?**
Likely. The developer states that Episode One's gameplay, story, and combat systems are complete and functional but "subject to change as new content is developed," with three more episodes planned before version 1.0.
