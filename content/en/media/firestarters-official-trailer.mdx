## The FIRESTARTERS Official Trailer, Explained in One Place

If you searched for the **FIRESTARTERS official trailer**, you probably want two things: the actual videos and a straight answer about what the game is. There are two trailer releases tied to Studio Kerosene's arena shooter — an announcement trailer and an Episode One trailer — and the game itself entered Early Access on PC on October 28, 2026 at $9.99. Below is what each trailer is confirmed to be, what it tells us about the game, and where the marketing stops and the verified store details begin.

## Two Different Trailers, Two Different Jobs

The confusion around the FIRESTARTERS official trailer is mostly a naming problem. Two separate videos circulate under similar titles, and they arrived at very different points in the game's rollout.

- **The announcement trailer** — a reveal video credited to Studio Kerosene. A Dailymotion upload of it was listed as nine months old when checked in early November 2026, which places the reveal around early 2026. It is also hosted on YouTube under the title "Firestarters - Official Announcement Trailer" on the GameTrailers channel.
- **The Episode One trailer** — a later, content-specific trailer covered by Gaming Hearts Collection on October 8, 2026, roughly three weeks before the Early Access launch.

The first video sells the concept. The second one arrives close enough to launch that it functions as a final content showcase for what buyers would actually get on day one. Neither is a deep-dive developer breakdown, so treat both as marketing rather than a feature list.

## What the Announcement Trailer Establishes

The announcement trailer's own description is unusually specific about positioning. It calls FIRESTARTERS a "high-speed cyberpunk arcade arena first-person shooter" from developer Studio Kerosene, and frames the premise as a government-sanctioned bloodsport where players skate, kill, and fight bloodthirsty androids for a shot at freedom. It also confirms the platform and window: PC, launching into Early Access in 2026.

That description lines up with the tone of the Steam listing, which describes the game as a fast-paced arcade movement shooter broken up by visual novel story cutscenes. If the trailer is your first exposure, the takeaway is that this is a movement-first shooter with a serialized narrative wrapper — not a military sim and not a pure arena deathmatch game.

One caution: the announcement trailer does not contain a final release date, a price, or a content roadmap. Those details only became concrete later in the cycle.

## What the Episode One Trailer Arrives With

By the time the Episode One trailer landed on October 8, 2026, the shape of the launch build was already public. According to the developer's Early Access notes on Steam, the initial release consists of Episode One, which includes the first part of the main Story Mode plus an unlockable Arcade Mode with global leaderboards.

That matters for how you read the trailer. Anything shown as part of the story content should be understood as Episode One material specifically, not the full four-episode plan. The developer intends to ship a total of four episodes before version 1.0, with an estimated 18 to 24 months in Early Access — a number the studio explicitly says may change.

In other words, the Episode One trailer is a snapshot of the launch build. It is not a trailer for the finished game.

## Reading the Trailers Against the Steam Page

Trailers are edited to look effortless; the store page is where the mechanics actually get described. Cross-referencing the two gives a much more useful picture of what the videos are showing.

**Movement is the pitch.** Kera, the player character, is a former delinquent street kid from Okinawa fighting on behalf of a team of prisoners called the Firestarters. Her kit is a STEEL Battlesuit, a pair of custom rocket skates, and an arena FPS weapon set. The arena is built to be skated, slid, ground-pounded, and ground through.

**Gunplay is self-sufficient.** There are six weapons in the arsenal, and no health, armor, or ammunition pickups anywhere in a STEEL Arena. Resources come from combat performance rather than map pickups.

**Scoring is the long tail.** After clearing a Storm for the first time in the story, players can replay it in Arcade Mode to post an official high score, compare against Steam friends, and push for the global leaderboard.

**The tech claim is unusual.** FIRESTARTERS runs on a fully custom engine built in-house, which the studio presents as a way to avoid shader compilation stutter, AI upscaling, and install bloat. That is a developer claim, not an independently verified benchmark.

For the full first-party description, the [FIRESTARTERS page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3733710/FIRESTARTERS/) is the authoritative source.
