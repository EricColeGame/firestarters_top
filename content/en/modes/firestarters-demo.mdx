## The FIRESTARTERS Demo Is Gone — Here's What Replaced It

If you went looking for the FIRESTARTERS demo this week, you won't find it. Studio Kerosene delisted the demo from Steam on October 28, 2026, the same day the game launched into Early Access. The studio's own notice explains the reasoning plainly: the demo had been live for a while, it had served its purpose, and it "no longer represents the quality of the game" shipping in the Early Access build.

That matters because the demo was, until launch day, the only free way to try Kera's rocket-skate combat. Now the paid Early Access version is the only way in — and it comes with two distinct modes rather than a short sample.

## Why Studio Kerosene Pulled the Demo

The announcement posted to Steam is short and direct. It thanks players, notes that the demo had been "active on Steam for some time," and states that the demo would be officially delisted alongside the Early Access launch. No replacement demo, trial, or free weekend was announced alongside it.

A few things are worth separating here:

- **The demo was not broken or cancelled mid-development.** The studio describes it as having "effectively served its purpose."
- **The delisting was planned to coincide with launch**, not a surprise takedown after the fact.
- **Nothing in the notice suggests the demo will return.** As of this writing, there's no public plan to re-list it.

Pre-launch coverage from outlets like MonsterVine and Kropp Media both pointed readers toward the demo as a try-before-you-buy option in early and late October. Those articles are now outdated on that specific point — the recommendation no longer applies.

## What Modes Are in the Early Access Version?

Episode One is the current Early Access build, and per the developer's own description it contains "both the first part of the main Story Mode, as well as the unlockable Arcade Mode." So the demo's replacement isn't a single slice — it's a story campaign plus a score-attack mode that unlocks from it.

### Story Mode

Story Mode is where you meet Kera, a former delinquent from Okinawa's back alleys who now fights as the designated competitor for the Firestarters, a team of prisoners in the bloodsport STEEL. The narrative is delivered through visual novel–style cutscenes between combat sequences.

Combat itself is arena shooter work built around movement: skating, sliding, grinding, and ground pounding through spaces that double as skateparks. Kera carries six weapons, and there are no health, armor, or ammo pickups in a STEEL arena — you take resources from defeated androids instead.

### Arcade Mode

Arcade Mode is the mode most people searching for a demo probably care about, because it's the replayable one. It unlocks after you clear a Storm for the first time in the story, and that same Storm then becomes an Arcade stage you can run for an official score.

The structure is straightforward: beat your personal best, then beat your Steam friends, then chase the global leaderboard. The Steam page frames it as the game's competitive backbone — spectators in-fiction demand faster kills and higher scores, and Arcade Mode is where that pressure becomes a number.

## How Arcade Mode Scoring Actually Works

Based on the developer's description, Arcade Mode is gated behind story progress rather than available from the first minute. You can't grind leaderboards on a Storm you haven't cleared in Story Mode yet.

That has a practical consequence for new buyers: expect to spend your first session in Story Mode before Arcade opens up. If your goal is purely score chasing, the unlock is the price of admission.

The scoring loop itself rewards aggression. Because there are no pickups, staying alive means killing and looting efficiently, and the game explicitly pushes toward faster clears rather than cautious ones. Whether that translates into combo multipliers, time bonuses, or another scoring layer isn't detailed in the material available — treat any specific scoring formula you see elsewhere as unconfirmed.

If you're the type who bounces off unfinished games, the studio's own advice applies: wait and see how development progresses. If you mainly want to know whether the movement and shooting feel right before committing, the demo used to answer that question — and it no longer can. The current substitute is the Early Access build itself, and at $9.99 the entry cost is low relative to waiting 18 to 24 months.

You can check current availability and system requirements on the [FIRESTARTERS Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3733710/FIRESTARTERS/). Minimum specs are modest — a 4-core CPU, 2 GB RAM, and 1 GB of storage — though the developer notes those requirements are not final.

## FAQ

**Can I still download the FIRESTARTERS demo?**

No. Studio Kerosene delisted the FIRESTARTERS demo from Steam on October 28, 2026, the same day the game entered Early Access. The studio said the demo no longer reflected the quality of the launch build. No replacement demo has been announced.

**Does the Early Access version include everything the demo had?**

The demo's exact contents were never itemized by the studio, so a direct comparison isn't possible. What's confirmed is that Episode One includes the first part of Story Mode plus the unlockable Arcade Mode, and the developer describes the base gameplay, story, and combat systems as complete and functional.

**Is Arcade Mode available right away?**

No. Arcade Mode unlocks after you complete a Storm for the first time in Story Mode. Once cleared, that Storm becomes available as an Arcade stage where you can set an official high score and compete on global leaderboards.

**Will the demo come back when the game leaves Early Access?**

Nothing in the studio's notice or Early Access FAQ mentions a returning demo. The stated plan is four episodes over roughly 18 to 24 months, with a gradual price increase as content ships and all pre-1.0 updates free for Early Access buyers.
