## Who Is Behind the FIRESTARTERS Game Studio?

If you went looking for the FIRESTARTERS game studio, the short answer is **Studio Kerosene** — the independent team behind the rocket-skate arena shooter that launched into Steam Early Access on October 28, 2026. The studio is small enough that its public footprint is concentrated in one place: the [official FIRESTARTERS Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3733710/FIRESTARTERS/), where the developers answer Early Access questions in their own words.

Lead developer Alex Harte has also spoken to press about the design intent. In MonsterVine's [launch coverage of the rocket-skate combat](https://monstervine.com/2026/10/firestarters-steam-early-access-october-28/), Harte framed the game's resource economy bluntly: there are no safety nets in the arenas of Steel, and hunting is the path to survival.

### The custom engine claim

Studio Kerosene says FIRESTARTERS runs on a fully custom engine built in-house from the ground up. According to the studio, that means no hard drive bloat, no AI upscaling, and no shader compilation stuttering. The team also states that no generative AI tools were used in the game's creation.

Those are the developer's claims rather than independently verified findings — treat them as promises to watch during Early Access, not as measured results.

## The Early Access Roadmap: Four Episodes Before 1.0

FIRESTARTERS is being built episodically. Per the Steam listing, the studio intends to ship **four "Episodes"** (major content releases) before version 1.0, while updating and improving existing content in between. The studio notes the episode count could change.

The current Early Access build is Episode One, which includes:

- The first part of the main Story Mode
- The unlockable Arcade Mode, where players chase high scores on global leaderboards
- Base gameplay, story, and combat systems the studio describes as complete and functional, though subject to change

The studio estimates Early Access will last roughly **18 to 24 months**, ending when the final story episode ships as version 1.0. No schedule for Episodes Two through Four has been announced.

### Pricing during Early Access

Studio Kerosene plans to gradually raise the price as new content and features arrive. Anyone who buys during Early Access receives all subsequent content updates before the full release at no extra cost. The Steam listing shows $9.99, and a hands-on preview from ColouredStaticPress reported a UK launch price of £6.99.

## How the Studio Says It Will Use Community Feedback

For a community-focused page, this is the part that matters most. In the Early Access Q&A on Steam, the developers pledge to read and consider any and all feedback and discussion they receive — through the Steam Forums or any other channels they can find, official or unofficial.

The studio's stated position is that nothing is more important to the game's success than the satisfaction of its player base, and that comments, complaints, and concerns are all wanted. Whether that translates into visible change is something players will judge episode by episode.

### Where the community actually gathers

- **Steam Forums** — the studio's named primary channel for feedback
- **Steam updates feed** — patch notes and announcements (the listing showed a latest update dated October 31, 2026)
- **Arcade Mode leaderboards** — the built-in competitive layer, including comparisons against Steam friends and a global board

A playable demo was available on Steam around the October 8 release-date announcement, giving players a low-cost way to test movement and gunplay before committing.

## What Early Hands-On Impressions Say

One outlet has published hands-on impressions, and it's worth labeling clearly: this is a single short session, not a review.

ColouredStaticPress published a [hands-on preview of the Steam Early Access build](https://colouredstaticpress.com/firestarters-hands-on-preview-steam-early-access-studio-kerosene/) on October 30, 2026. The writer described a momentum-driven rhythm — arenas of rails, ramps, and sharp turns that are designed to be traversed rather than used as cover — and called the scoring system a genuine reason to retry a stage rather than treat it as a checklist of targets.

The same preview flagged one concern: in the red industrial arenas, a dense HUD, stark effects, and overlapping warnings can compete for the eye at high speed. Readability, not spectacle, is the thing to watch.
