## What FIRESTARTERS Actually Asks of a New Player

FIRESTARTERS hit Steam Early Access on October 28, 2026, and it is not a game that rewards caution. Studio Kerosene describes it as a fast-paced arcade movement shooter with visual novel story cutscenes, and the arena design backs that up: you are meant to be skating, sliding, grinding, and ground-pounding through every space rather than holding an angle behind cover.

That single design choice shapes almost every beginner mistake. If you come in playing it like a conventional arena shooter — stop, aim, clear the room — you will stall out. This FIRESTARTERS beginner guide breaks down what the game tells you directly, what Early Access currently contains, and which habits to build in your first few Storms.

One warning before anything else: the developers include a photosensitivity notice on the store page, citing flashing lights and stroboscopic screen effects. Read it before your first session.

## Start With Movement, Not Aim

### Learn the skates before you learn the guns

Kera's kit is built around a performance-enhancing STEEL Battlesuit and a custom pair of rocket skates. The Steam page frames this as "best-in-class character movement," and the arenas are explicitly described as skateparks rather than shooting galleries. Rails, ramps, and sharp turns are the terrain — Coloured Static Press noted in its hands-on preview that both the bright training spaces and the red-lit industrial interiors are "designed to be traversed rather than treated as rooms in which to take cover."

Practical first steps:

- Spend your opening runs testing skate, slide, grind, and ground pound in the brighter practice-style arenas before worrying about score.
- Treat rails and ramps as your default route through a space, not as optional shortcuts.
- Practice chaining a grind into a shot so the two actions stop feeling separate.

### Expect the camera and HUD to fight you at speed

The same hands-on preview flagged a readability concern: dense HUD elements, stark effects, and multiple simultaneous warnings can compete for your attention once the pace picks up. As a beginner, that is worth planning around. If the on-screen noise is overwhelming, slow your route down deliberately for a few runs and learn one arena's layout at a time instead of chasing speed immediately.

## How Combat Works When There Are No Pickups

This is the mechanic that catches most new players off guard. There are no health, armor, or ammunition pickups in a STEEL arena. None. The Steam listing is blunt about it — if the world does not give, you take — and lead developer Alex Harte put it this way in the launch announcement coverage: "There are no safety nets in the arenas of Steel. Hunting is the path to survival."

In practice, that means your resources come from defeated androids. Six weapons are available in Kera's arsenal, and the flow of a run depends on pushing forward into the enemy horde rather than retreating to recover.

Beginner habits that follow from this:

- **Kill to reload.** When you are low, your answer is the next enemy, not a corner of the map.
- **Do not backtrack.** The arena has nothing waiting for you behind you.
- **Watch the mechanized claws.** The Steam page specifically calls out the androids' savage mechanical claws as the thing to avoid while moving.
- **Keep the route circular.** Momentum plus a predictable loop beats a frantic scramble every time.

## Understanding Storms, Arcade Mode, and the Score Chase

Clearing an encounter is only half the job. The game's fiction is STEEL, a livestreamed gladiator bloodsport run by a crime syndicate, and the audience in that fiction demands spectacle — faster kills, higher scores, no stagnation.

Here is the structure as currently documented:

1. Play through a Storm in Story Mode for the first time.
2. Once cleared, that Storm becomes available in Arcade Mode.
3. Arcade Mode is where official high scores are recorded, with Steam friends and global leaderboards to chase.

A hands-on session from Coloured Static Press described the game tracking remaining enemies, score, and multipliers on screen, with a synchronization meter visible in the HUD. Treat that as an observation from one preview build, not a guaranteed final system — the developers have said the base gameplay, story, and combat systems are functional but subject to change as new content ships.

For a beginner, the useful takeaway is sequencing: finish the Storm first, then return to it in Arcade Mode once you know the layout. Chasing a high score on a map you have never seen is a waste of a run.

## FAQ

**Is there a health pickup system I should learn as a beginner?**
No. FIRESTARTERS deliberately omits health, armor, and ammo pickups from its arenas. A FIRESTARTERS beginner guide that tells you to memorize pickup spawns is describing a different game — your recovery loop is defeating androids.

**Do I have to finish the story before I can chase high scores?**
Yes, at least per Storm. A Storm must be cleared once in Story Mode before it unlocks in Arcade Mode, where official high scores and global leaderboard placements are tracked.

**How much does the game cost right now, and will it go up?**
It launched at $9.99 on Steam and £6.99 in the UK. Studio Kerosene has said the price will rise gradually as new episodes and features ship, and that anyone who buys during Early Access gets all pre-1.0 content updates free.

**Is the game finished?**
No. Early Access began with Episode One of a planned four, and the studio estimates 18 to 24 months before version 1.0. Story Mode, combat, and the core systems are described as complete and functional, but the developers say they remain subject to change.
