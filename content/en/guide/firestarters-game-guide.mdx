## Start Here: What FIRESTARTERS Actually Is

If you are searching for a FIRESTARTERS game guide, the first thing worth knowing is that this is not a loot-driven shooter where you scavenge the map for supplies. It is a fast arcade movement shooter built around momentum, aggressive hunting, and score chasing — and it entered Steam Early Access on October 28, 2026 with only its first episode.

That matters because most guides written today describe an incomplete game. Studio Kerosene's own Early Access notes state that Episode One contains the opening portion of Story Mode plus an unlockable Arcade Mode, and that the base gameplay, story, and combat systems are already functional but subject to change. The developer estimates 18 to 24 months in Early Access before version 1.0, with four total "Episodes" planned.

So treat everything below as a snapshot of Episode One, verified against the [official FIRESTARTERS Steam listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3733710/FIRESTARTERS/) and launch-window coverage.

### The setup in one paragraph

You play as Kera, a former delinquent from the back alleys of Okinawa who is now a prisoner competing in STEEL, a Japanese gladiator bloodsport livestreamed by a crime syndicate. She is the designated fighter for the Firestarters, a team of inmates fighting for their freedom. Between battles, visual-novel-style cutscenes carry the story.

## Movement First: Skate, Slide, Grind, Ground Pound

The Steam page describes the arenas of STEEL as a skatepark: you skate, slide, grind, and ground pound through each space while avoiding mechanized claws. Kera's kit is a performance-enhancing STEEL Battlesuit plus a custom pair of rocket skates.

A hands-on preview from [Coloured Static Press](https://colouredstaticpress.com/firestarters-hands-on-preview-steam-early-access-studio-kerosene/) describes arenas built from rails, ramps, and sharp turns, split between bright training spaces and red-lit industrial interiors. Crucially, the preview notes these areas are designed to be traversed, not used as cover. If you play this like a stop-and-pop shooter, you are fighting the design.

Practical takeaways you can act on now:

- **Keep moving between kills.** The scoring system rewards sustained pace, so chain encounters rather than clearing a room and pausing.
- **Learn rail lines before you need them.** Grinding and sliding are traversal tools, not just flourishes.
- **Expect to replay arenas.** The preview describes the scoring loop as the reason to try again rather than treating each arena as a checklist of targets.

Exact keybinds and input mappings are not documented in the available material, so check the in-game control menu rather than relying on secondhand bindings.

## The Combat Loop: No Health, Armor, or Ammo Pickups

This is the single most important rule to internalize. According to the Steam page, you will find no health, armor, or ammunition pickups in a STEEL arena. Resources come from defeated androids instead — you take what you need from what you kill.

Lead developer Alex Harte framed it bluntly in launch coverage: there are no safety nets in the arenas, and hunting is the path to survival. That single design choice reshapes how you should approach every fight.

- **Aggression is defense.** Backing away from enemies starves you of the resources that retreating would protect.
- **Kill order matters more than aim.** Prioritize the enemy you can reach and finish, not the one that looks threatening.
- **Six weapons, self-sufficient gunplay.** The store page lists an arsenal of six weapons; the intent is that you never depend on pickups to keep firing.
- **Do not hoard.** Since ammo comes from kills, sitting on a full magazine while avoiding contact is a net loss.

## Arcade Mode and the Score Chase

Episode One's structure is straightforward: clear a Storm in Story Mode for the first time, then return to that same Storm in Arcade Mode to set an official high score.

The Steam page is explicit that clearing an encounter is only part of the challenge — spectators want faster kills and higher scores. The preview build tracked remaining enemies, score, and multipliers on screen, with a synchronization meter visible in captured footage. Because leaderboards are global, the scoring layer is where the long-tail competition lives.

What that means in practice:

1. Finish Story Mode clears first to unlock Arcade entries.
2. Use early Arcade attempts to learn spawn flow rather than chasing a perfect score.
3. Compare against your own best, then Steam friends, then the global board — the developer frames progression in exactly that order.

## PC Requirements and Readability Warnings

The system requirements are unusually light, though the developer labels them non-final:

- **Minimum:** Windows 10, 4-core CPU (Intel i3 or AMD Ryzen 3), 2 GB RAM, DirectX 12, 1 GB storage
- **Recommended:** Windows 11, 6-core CPU (Intel i5 or AMD Ryzen 5), 2 GB RAM, 1 GB storage

One practical caution comes from the Coloured Static Press hands-on session: in the striking red industrial arenas, a dense HUD, stark effects, and multiple simultaneous warnings can compete for your attention when the action accelerates. Their suggestion was that readability of the arena itself needs to keep pace with the clear score feedback. If you find yourself losing track of threats, that is a design tension in the current build rather than a personal failing.

Also note the store page carries a photosensitivity warning for flashing lights and stroboscopic effects, and a mature content description covering blood, intense violence, strong language, mature and suggestive themes, and tobacco use.

## FAQ

**Is there a FIRESTARTERS game guide for weapon unlocks?**
Not yet, and the verified material does not describe a weapon unlock system. The Steam page references an arsenal of six weapons without detailing progression, so any unlock list you see online right now is unconfirmed.

**Do I need to finish Story Mode before playing Arcade Mode?**
Yes. Arcade Mode is unlocked content, and the Steam page states you return to a Storm in Arcade Mode after completing it for the first time in the story.

**Where do I get health and ammo?**
From defeated androids. There are no health, armor, or ammunition pickups placed in STEEL arenas, which is why aggressive play is the intended survival strategy.

**Is the game finished?**
No. Episode One is the initial Early Access release, with three further episodes planned as free updates for Early Access owners before version 1.0.
