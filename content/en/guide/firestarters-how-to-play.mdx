## FIRESTARTERS How to Play: The One Rule That Governs Everything

If you only remember one thing about playing FIRESTARTERS, make it this: standing still is how you lose. Studio Kerosene's arcade movement shooter puts you in rocket skates inside arenas built like skateparks, then removes every safety net the genre normally hands you — no health packs, no armor pickups, no ammo crates. Everything you need to survive comes out of the androids you destroy, which means your "how to play" plan is really a "how to keep hunting" plan. This guide covers what the game actually asks of you, based on the official Steam listing and hands-on press coverage from the October 2026 Early Access launch.

## Before You Drop In: What You're Actually Playing

FIRESTARTERS launched in Steam Early Access on October 28, 2026, as an action/indie title from Studio Kerosene, priced at $9.99 USD at the time of writing. The current build is **Episode One**, which contains the opening stretch of Story Mode plus an unlockable Arcade Mode. The developers plan four episodes total before version 1.0, with an estimated 18 to 24 months in Early Access.

A few practical notes before your first run:

- **Platform:** PC only for now, with Windows 10 (minimum) or Windows 11 (recommended), a 4- to 6-core CPU, 2 GB RAM, DirectX 12, and about 1 GB of storage. The developers label these requirements non-final.
- **Demo:** A playable Steam demo was offered ahead of launch, per Noisy Pixel's October 7, 2026 report — worth checking if you want a feel for the skates before buying.
- **Accessibility:** The store page carries a photosensitivity warning for flashing lights and stroboscopic effects, plus a mature content label for blood, intense violence, strong language, and tobacco use.
- **Controls:** Neither the Steam page nor the previews document a full keybind list, so treat the in-game tutorial and options menu as your authority rather than any third-party cheat sheet.

## Movement First: Skate, Slide, Grind, Ground Pound

Kera's kit is a performance-boosting STEEL Battlesuit paired with custom-built rocket skates, and the arenas are explicitly designed to be traversed rather than used as cover. The official description lists the core movement verbs as skating, sliding, grinding, and ground pounding. Press hands-on impressions from Coloured Static describe arenas full of rails, ramps, and sharp turns, with runs moving between bright training spaces and red-lit industrial interiors.

What that means in practice:

- **Chain your verbs.** Grinds and slides aren't flourishes; they're how you cover ground while staying hard to hit. Look for rails as route options, not decoration.
- **Use verticality.** Platforms and ramps exist to keep you above the mechanized claws rather than in front of them.
- **Ground pound as a commit, not a default.** It's a listed ability, but nothing in the available material details its damage or cooldown — test it in a safe arena before relying on it.
- **Don't treat arenas as shooting galleries.** The moment you stop to line up shots from a static position, you give enemies time to close.

## Combat: Six Weapons and Zero Pickups

Kera carries an arsenal of six weapons, and the store page describes the gunplay as "fully self-sufficient" — no ammo economy to manage beyond what you take from kills. There are no health, armor, or ammunition pickups anywhere in a STEEL arena. Defeated androids drop what you need, so aggression is the resource engine.

Lead developer Alex Harte put it bluntly in the launch coverage: there are no safety nets in STEEL's arenas, and hunting is the path to survival. Practically, that reframes combat decisions:

- **Kill to heal, not to clear.** Every android you leave alive is a resource you haven't collected.
- **Rotate targets instead of retreating.** Backing away from a fight starves you of both score and sustain.
- **Learn which weapon fits which spacing.** With six options and no ammo scarcity, the limiting factor is how well you pair a gun with your current speed and angle — not whether you can afford to fire.
- **Expect the systems to shift.** The developers state that base gameplay, story, and combat systems are functional but subject to change as episodes ship.

## Story Mode and Arcade Mode: How Progression Works

Episode One splits into a narrative campaign told through visual-novel cutscenes and a score-attack mode that opens up as you go. Clearing a Storm — the game's term for a combat encounter — in the story unlocks that Storm for Arcade replay, where your performance is logged to leaderboards.

| Feature | Story Mode | Arcade Mode |
|---|---|---|
| What you do | Play the opening chapter of Kera's story, with visual-novel cutscenes between fights | Replay completed Storms for score |
| Availability | Included in Episode One | Unlockable; Storms unlock after their first story clear |
| Progression | Advances the narrative | Personal bests, Steam friends comparison, global leaderboard placement |
| Pressure | Survive and finish | Kill faster, score higher, satisfy the crowd |

The audience framing isn't flavor text — the store page is explicit that clearing a Storm is the minimum, and that spectators expect competitors to play better and slay faster. Arcade Mode is where that expectation becomes a number.

## Reading the Arena When the HUD Gets Loud

One caveat from hands-on play is worth carrying into your own sessions. Coloured Static's preview noted that the red industrial arenas have a strong visual identity, but a dense HUD, stark effects, and multiple simultaneous warnings can compete for your attention once the pace picks up. The score feedback is clear; arena readability is the thing to watch.

If you find yourself losing track of threats, adjust rather than push through:

- Lower or simplify visual settings if the options allow it.
- Prioritize the remaining-enemy and multiplier readouts, since those drive your route.
- Learn arena layouts in Story Mode, where the stakes are lower than a leaderboard run.

That feedback reflects a short hands-on session, not a final verdict on the game.

## FAQ

**Is there a FIRESTARTERS how-to-play tutorial in the game?**
The store page and launch coverage don't detail a tutorial sequence. The safest approach is to start in Story Mode, which introduces the opening chapter and lets you learn movement and combat before chasing scores in Arcade Mode.

**Do I need to finish Story Mode before playing Arcade Mode?**
Arcade Mode is described as unlockable in Episode One, and individual Storms become available for Arcade replay after you complete them in the story for the first time. Expect to play some campaign first.

**How do I heal in FIRESTARTERS?**
You don't pick up health. There are no health, armor, or ammo pickups in STEEL arenas — resources come from defeated androids, so staying aggressive is the sustain mechanic.

**Does the FIRESTARTERS how-to-play loop change with future episodes?**
The developers plan four episodes before version 1.0 and say existing gameplay, story, and combat systems may change as new content ships. Treat any current guide, this one included, as accurate to Episode One in November 2026.
