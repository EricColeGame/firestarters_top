## Movement First: Skate, Slide, Grind, and Ground Pound

The official store description names the four movement verbs you need to internalize: skate, slide, grind, and ground pound. Arenas are built from rails, ramps, and sharp turns, and a hands-on preview from [Coloured Static Press](https://colouredstaticpress.com/firestarters-hands-on-preview-steam-early-access-studio-kerosene/) described spaces designed to be traversed rather than treated as rooms to take cover in. That single design choice reshapes everything else.

If you play FIRESTARTERS like a conventional cover shooter, you will lose tempo and, eventually, the run. Standing still costs you the two things the game actually rewards: survivability through motion and a score worth posting.

Three habits worth building early:

- **Chain your transitions.** Land a slide into a grind instead of stopping between them; the arenas are laid out to keep a line going.
- **Use verticality.** Platforms and ramps are part of the route, not scenery.
- **Ground pound as a tool, not a finisher.** It is listed alongside the other movement options, so treat it as part of your routing vocabulary.

The developer's own framing is that Kera's battlesuit, rocket skates, and weapon suite are meant to deliver "best-in-class character movement." Movement is the skill ceiling here.

## Combat and Resources: Six Weapons, Zero Pickups

This is the mechanic that catches most new players. There are no health, armor, or ammunition pickups placed around a STEEL arena. Everything you need comes from the androids you destroy. Lead developer Alex Harte framed it bluntly in the launch announcement: the arenas have no safety nets, and hunting is how you survive.

Practically, that turns aggression into an ammo economy:

- **Kill to reload.** If you are low on resources, the answer is a new target, not a retreat.
- **Do not over-commit to one weapon.** With six weapons in the kit, spreading usage keeps you from stalling out when one runs dry.
- **Keep moving between kills.** Standing still to "conserve" resources is the opposite of how the loop works.

You are also avoiding what the store page calls the savage mechanized claws of your enemies — another reason to stay on rails and ramps rather than planting your feet.

## Scoring: How Storms and Arcade Mode Fit Together

The store page refers to clearing a "Storm," the game's term for a combat encounter you fight through. Finishing a Storm in Story Mode is not the end of it. Once you clear one for the first time in the story, you can return to it in Arcade Mode and set an official high score, which feeds into global leaderboards and comparisons with your Steam friends.

The preview build tracked remaining enemies, score, and multipliers on screen, which tells you what the game is measuring: not just whether you survived, but how fast and how cleanly.

Two scoring notes from hands-on reporting:

- **Pace beats caution.** The preview described momentum as the core of a good run, with retries driven by chasing a better score rather than clearing a checklist.
- **Watch the HUD clutter.** The same preview flagged that dense HUD elements, stark effects, and overlapping warnings can compete for your attention during fast sequences. If you are losing track of the arena, that is a readability issue worth adjusting around, not a personal failing.

## FAQ

**Is there a complete FIRESTARTERS walkthrough available?**

Not yet. The game is in Early Access with only Episode One released as of October 28, 2026, and the developer plans four episodes before version 1.0. Any FIRESTARTERS walkthrough published today can only cover the launch build's systems — movement, resource stealing, and scoring — rather than a full campaign route.

**How do I get health and ammo in FIRESTARTERS?**

You don't pick them up. The arenas contain no health, armor, or ammunition pickups. Resources come from defeating android enemies, which is why aggressive, fast play is the intended survival strategy rather than a risky option.

**What is Arcade Mode and when does it unlock?**

Arcade Mode lets you replay a Storm you have already cleared in Story Mode to set an official high score and compete on global leaderboards. You unlock access to it by completing that Storm in the story first.

**Does FIRESTARTERS run on Steam Deck?**

Steam Deck compatibility is listed as "Unknown" on the official store page, so there is no verified status yet.
