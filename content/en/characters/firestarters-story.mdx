The FIRESTARTERS story follows a single fighter through a televised death sport: Kera, a former street delinquent from Okinawa who becomes the designated competitor for a prison team called the Firestarters. Studio Kerosene delivers that narrative through visual novel-style cutscenes between high-speed arena battles, and only the first chapter is currently available. Episode One launched in Steam Early Access on October 28, 2026, with four episodes planned in total before version 1.0.

## The FIRESTARTERS Story at a Glance

The premise is compact, and the official [FIRESTARTERS page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3733710/FIRESTARTERS/) lays out the essentials without much ambiguity. Here is what the game itself confirms about its narrative setup.

Everything in that table comes from the developer's own store description or its Early Access roadmap answers. Details beyond it — teammate names, syndicate leadership, and where Kera's arc ends — are not yet part of the public record.

## Who Is Kera, the Face of the FIRESTARTERS Story?

Kera is the player character and the emotional center of the narrative. Before her conviction, she was a formidable delinquent street kid surviving in the back alleys of Okinawa. That background matters, because the game frames her current situation as a consequence of it rather than an accident.

Her role in STEEL is specific: she is the designated fighter for the Firestarters, described as the sport's newest team of prisoners. She is not a volunteer, a celebrity, or a legacy competitor. She is an inmate handed a battlesuit and a pair of rocket skates and told to entertain an audience.

The game gives her two motives, and both are personal. She fights to earn freedom for the friends she has found on her team, and she fights as atonement for her past sins. That combination — loyalty to new allies plus guilt over old behavior — is the hook the story hangs on.

Her kit reinforces the character rather than contradicting her. A performance-enhancing STEEL Battlesuit, custom-built rocket skates, and a curated set of arena weapons make her a mobile, aggressive fighter. In story terms, she is someone who survives by never standing still, which fits a protagonist running from her own history.

## What Is STEEL, and Why Does It Matter to the Story?

STEEL is the world's top-rated livestream in the game's future, and it is a Japanese gladiator bloodsport owned and operated by the nation's most notorious crime syndicate. That single sentence does a lot of narrative work: the sport is entertainment first, competition second, and the people running it are criminals rather than regulators.

The crowd's appetite shapes the plot. The game states plainly that spectators will not tolerate stagnation, and that clearing an encounter is not enough — competitors are expected to kill faster and score higher. A champion is worth more to the broadcast than a survivor.

That pressure explains why the arenas function as skateparks instead of cover-shooter corridors. Rails, ramps, and sharp turns are there to be traversed, and the enemies are an android horde with savage mechanized claws rather than human opponents with grudges. The violence is stylized but explicitly mature: the developer's content description lists blood, intense violence, strong language, mature and suggestive themes, and tobacco use.

## How the FIRESTARTERS Story Is Told

Studio Kerosene has been upfront that this is a hybrid project. The studio describes FIRESTARTERS as a fast-paced arcade movement shooter with visual novel story cutscenes, which means the narrative arrives in dialogue-driven scenes between combat storms rather than through environmental storytelling or audio logs.

Coverage of the Early Access launch supports that framing. [MonsterVine's report on the October 28 launch](https://monstervine.com/2026/10/firestarters-steam-early-access-october-28/) notes that visual novel cutscenes tell the story between battles, and [Noisy Pixel's announcement coverage](https://noisypixel.net/firestarters-steam-early-access-october-28/) describes the same structure, with cutscenes expanding on Kera and the wider STEEL competition.

A hands-on preview from [Coloured Static Press](https://colouredstaticpress.com/firestarters-hands-on-preview-steam-early-access-studio-kerosene/) adds a reader-facing observation: the visual-novel exchanges introduce the characters and give the project personality beyond its mechanical influences, and the writer wants to see whether later episodes develop those relationships and stakes. That is an impression from a short session, not a verdict on the finished story.

## The Cast Beyond Kera — and What Remains Unnamed

Here is where the available material sets a hard limit. Kera is the only named story character in the developer's official description, and no teammate, syndicate boss, or rival fighter is identified by name in any of the launch coverage reviewed here.

What we can say with confidence:

- The Firestarters are a team of prisoners, and Kera's stated goal involves winning freedom for the friends she has made among them.
- The syndicate that owns STEEL is described as the nation's most notorious crime syndicate, but it is not named.
- The enemies Kera fights are androids, described as a horde, not individual characters with dialogue.
- Alex Harte is credited as lead developer in launch coverage — a real member of the development team, not a character in the fiction.

That gap is worth naming honestly. Anyone looking for a full character roster with bios and relationships will not find one yet, because Episode One is the opening chapter of a story planned to unfold across roughly 18 to 24 months of Early Access.

## What Episode One Establishes, and What Comes Next

Episode One contains the first part of the main Story Mode along with the unlockable Arcade Mode, where players chase high scores on global leaderboards. The developers describe the base gameplay, story, and combat systems as complete and functional, while noting they remain subject to change as more content is built.

Three further episodes are planned as free updates for anyone who buys in during Early Access. The studio has also said the price will rise gradually as new content ships — the Steam listing showed $9.99 at the time of writing, and Coloured Static Press reported a UK launch price of £6.99. Regional pricing varies, so treat those figures as launch-window snapshots rather than fixed facts.

For story purposes, the practical takeaway is simple: Episode One sets up Kera's sentence, her team, and the machinery of STEEL. The payoff — how her atonement arc resolves, who her teammates really are, and what the syndicate wants from her — has not been released yet.

## FAQ

**Who is the main character in the FIRESTARTERS story?**
Kera, a former delinquent from the back alleys of Okinawa who is now a prisoner. She serves as the designated fighter for the Firestarters, STEEL's newest team of inmates, and fights both to free her new friends and to atone for her past.

**Is the FIRESTARTERS story complete?**
No. The game entered Steam Early Access on October 28, 2026, with Episode One, which covers the first part of Story Mode. Studio Kerosene plans four episodes in total before the 1.0 release and estimates 18 to 24 months in Early Access, a timeline the studio notes may change.

**Does FIRESTARTERS use cutscenes to tell its story?**
Yes. The developer describes the game as an arcade movement shooter with visual novel story cutscenes, and launch coverage confirms that dialogue-driven scenes play between combat encounters rather than during them.

**What is STEEL in the FIRESTARTERS story?**
STEEL is a Japanese gladiator bloodsport and the world's top-rated livestream in the game's future setting. It is owned and operated by a notorious crime syndicate, and its audience demands spectacle — faster kills, higher scores, and champions worth watching.
